Lesson 1: The Vertex Form of a Quadratic Function

Look at the streams

Look at the water leaving the fountain. Each stream goes up and comes down.

Choose one stream. Name the highest point of that stream, the place the water does not go above. Say where it is on the photograph. The photograph cannot be marked. Do not measure, and do not write an equation yet.

That turning point is the fact we will want from an equation of the curve. Vertex form makes the high or low point readable.

A quadratic written as

f(x) = a(x - h)² + k

is in vertex form. The vertex is the point (h, k). The axis of symmetry is the vertical line x = h. The sign of a tells you whether that vertex is a maximum or a minimum: negative a opens downward, so the vertex is a highest point; positive a opens upward, so the vertex is a lowest point.

Consider f(x) = (x + 3)² + 1. The equation is already in vertex form, but notice the sign inside the brackets.

f(x) = (x + 3)² + 1

Name the vertex and the axis of symmetry. Do not sketch yet.

In this course, f(x) = a(x - h)² + k is vertex form. The expanded equation f(x) = ax² + bx + c is standard form.

Read the turning point from the equation

You named a vertex from a plus inside the brackets. Read this next equation the same way: vertex, axis, and whether the turning point is a maximum or a minimum.

Consider

y = -3(x + 2)² + 4

Name the vertex. Is the vertex a maximum or a minimum? How did you know?

Compare the equation with y = a(x - h)² + k.

The brackets contain (x + 2). Vertex form uses (x - h), so rewrite x + 2 as x - (-2). That makes h = -2. The constant outside the brackets is +4, so k = 4.

The vertex is (-2, 4). The axis of symmetry is the vertical line through the vertex, x = -2.

The coefficient in front of the brackets is -3. Because a is negative, the parabola opens downward, so the vertex is a maximum. The maximum value of the function is 4, and it occurs when x = -2.

That highest point is the same kind of fact you named on the water stream, now read from the equation.

A second equation, with nothing added outside

The last equation had a constant outside the brackets, so k was easy to see as a height. Consider

h(x) = -2(x + 1)²

The brackets contain (x + 1). Rewrite x + 1 as x - (-1), so h = -1. There is no constant added outside, so k = 0. The vertex is (-1, 0), and the axis is x = -1.

The coefficient is -2, so a is negative and the parabola opens downward. The vertex is a maximum even though k = 0. Maximum versus minimum comes from the sign of a, not from whether k looks positive.

Where is the vertex? Does this graph have a maximum or a minimum? Could you decide that from k alone?

The same curve, two forms

Vertex form makes the turning point easy to read. Expanding that form into ax² + bx + c should not move the curve.

Rewrite

g(x) = (1)/(2)(x + 2)² - 3

in standard form, meaning g(x) = ax² + bx + c. Then check two x-values in both forms.

Square the binomial: (x + 2)² = x² + 4x + 4.

Multiply by a = (1)/(2): (1)/(2)x² + 2x + 2.

Add k = -3: (1)/(2)x² + 2x - 1.

g(x) = (1)/(2)x² + 2x - 1

At x = -2, both forms give -3. At x = 0, both give -1. Those table points sit on the same graph: expanding did not make a new parabola.

What vertex form is for

Consider f(x) = a(x - h)² + k. What can you read from vertex form that is harder to see when the same function is written in standard form f(x) = ax² + bx + c? Do not rewrite the function.

From vertex form you can read the vertex (h, k), the axis x = h, and whether the vertex is a maximum or a minimum from the sign of a. Standard form makes the y-intercept c easy to see. Zeros are not obvious in either of those two forms unless they happen to sit on an axis.

Factored form f(x) = a(x - r)(x - s) makes the x-intercepts easy to see. Vertex form does not make those intercepts as obvious, and the vertex is not an intercept unless it actually sits on an axis.

You need the highest point of a quadratic path, not the x-intercepts. Which of standard form, vertex form, or factored form would you choose, and why? Mention what that form does not make obvious.

Zeros from vertex form

Intercepts are not readable from vertex form the way they are from factored form. You can still find zeros by undoing the operations in the equation.

The graph of f(x) = (x - 1)² - 9 meets the x-axis where f(x) = 0. Determine those zeros by inverse operations. You may check on a graph afterward.

What vertex form gives you

Vertex form f(x) = a(x - h)² + k makes the vertex (h, k) and the axis x = h readable. Maximum versus minimum comes from the sign of a, not from the sign of k and not from which quadrant the vertex sits in.

Match the brackets to (x - h). A plus inside the brackets does not mean the vertex is on the positive side of the x-axis.

a stretches or flips the graph. k moves the vertex up or down. Those two jobs are not the same.

Expanding vertex form into f(x) = ax² + bx + c gives the same parabola, not a new one. The graphs should land on top of each other.
