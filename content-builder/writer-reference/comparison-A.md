## 1. Opening

A quadratic graph can model a path, an arch, or any situation with a highest or lowest point. Finding that turning point quickly helps you answer questions such as when a height reaches its maximum or where a minimum occurs.

One useful way to write a quadratic is **vertex form**:
\[
f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k.
\]
In this form, the vertex is \((h,k)\). The value of \(h\) gives its horizontal position, \(k\) gives its vertical position, and the sign of \(a\) tells you whether the parabola opens upward or downward.

Before graphing, look at
\[
f(x)=(x+3)^2+1.
\]
Predict the vertex and decide whether the graph opens upward or downward. Pay close attention to how \(x+3\) fits the pattern \(x-h\). Record your prediction and one detail from the equation that supports it. Then graph the function and compare the turning point with your prediction.

## 2. Worked example

Consider
\[
y=-3(x+2)^2+4.
\]

Compare it with vertex form, \(y=a(x-h)^2+k\).

Because \(x+2=x-(-2)\), the value of \(h\) is \(-2\). The value of \(k\) is \(4\). Therefore, the vertex is
\[
(-2,4).
\]

The coefficient \(a=-3\) is negative, so the parabola opens downward. Its vertex is therefore the highest point on the graph. The function has a maximum value of \(4\), reached when \(x=-2\).
